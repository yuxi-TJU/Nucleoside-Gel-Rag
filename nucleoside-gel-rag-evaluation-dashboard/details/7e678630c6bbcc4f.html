Based on the principles of nucleoside-derived supramolecular assembly, the gelation of guanosine derivatives is highly dependent on the ability of the nucleoside to form G-quartets (or similar tetrad/pentad structures) stabilized by specific cations.

### Analysis of Deoxyguanosine (dG)
Deoxyguanosine (dG) is known to self-assemble into G-quartet structures. However, the stability and morphology of these assemblies are strictly governed by the templating cation. 

1.  **Structural Requirements:** G-quartet formation requires the coordination of a central cation within the cavity formed by the O6 carbonyl oxygens of the guanine bases.
2.  **Cation Selectivity:** Potassium ions ($K^+$) are widely recognized as the optimal template for G-quartet stacking due to their ionic radius, which allows them to sit perfectly within the plane of the quartet, coordinating with the eight O6 oxygens of two stacked quartets.
3.  **The Role of Sodium ($Na^+$):** While $Na^+$ can coordinate with G-quartets, it is generally too small to effectively bridge the O6 carbonyls in the same manner as $K^+$. In the context of deoxyguanosine (dG) specifically, $Na^+$ often fails to promote the long-range, interconnected fiber network required for macroscopic hydrogel formation in aqueous media. Instead, dG in the presence of $Na^+$ typically results in a solution state or rapid precipitation/crystallization, as the lack of optimal templating prevents the formation of the stable, high-aspect-ratio supramolecular polymers necessary for gelation.

Therefore, under the specified conditions (1.4 mg / 100 µL dG in 0.2 M NaCl), dG is expected to remain in a solution or precipitate state rather than forming a stable hydrogel.
