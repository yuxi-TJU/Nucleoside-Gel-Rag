To determine the gelation potential of 2′,3′,5′-tri-O-acetylguanosine (TAcG), we must consider the structural requirements for guanosine-based supramolecular gelation. 

Guanosine derivatives typically form gels through the self-assembly of G-quartets, which are stabilized by Hoogsteen hydrogen bonding. This process usually requires the presence of specific cations (like $K^+$ or $Na^+$) to coordinate within the central cavity of the G-quartet stack. However, the formation of these quartets relies heavily on the availability of the hydrogen-bonding donors and acceptors on the guanine base. In TAcG, the 2', 3', and 5' hydroxyl groups are protected by acetyl groups. While this modification increases the lipophilicity of the molecule, it significantly alters the solubility profile and the potential for intermolecular hydrogen bonding compared to native guanosine.

Research into acetylated guanosine derivatives indicates that while they can exhibit interesting self-assembly properties, the tri-acetylated form (TAcG) often fails to form stable hydrogels or organogels under standard conditions because the bulky acetyl groups disrupt the necessary stacking interactions and the solubility balance required for long-range supramolecular polymerization. Specifically, in the presence of KCl, TAcG tends to precipitate or remain in solution rather than forming a stable, thermoreversible gel network, as the acetyl groups prevent the formation of the extended, ordered G-quartet columns typically seen in unprotected or selectively protected guanosine derivatives.
