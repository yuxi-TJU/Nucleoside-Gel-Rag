Output

The target molecule is uridine, which possesses a cis-2′,3′-diol on the ribose (identical to cytidine) and can therefore form dynamic borate diester cross-links with B(OH)₃ under basic conditions. However, macroscopic hydrogelation in the cytidine system also requires a second, orthogonal interaction: Ag⁺-mediated base-pairing (C·Ag⁺·C) that drives the formation of one-dimensional stacks that ultimately bundle into the percolating nanofibre network.  

Uridine contains a uracil rather than a cytosine nucleobase. Uracil lacks the specific donor–acceptor pattern that enables stable Ag⁺ coordination between two bases; consequently the Ag⁺-driven stacking column does not form. This situation is directly analogous to the behaviour of 5-fluorouridine (reference 3), which likewise fails to gel with either AgNO₃ or borate alone or in combination. The arabino-cytidine control (reference 2) further underscores that both interactions are indispensable: removal of either the borate-diester or the Ag⁺-base-pairing motif abolishes gelation.  

Because the Ag⁺-mediated supramolecular element is missing, the orthogonal cross-linking required for a self-supporting hydrogel cannot occur. The mixture therefore remains a free-flowing solution.
