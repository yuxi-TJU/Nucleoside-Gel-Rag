The target molecule (ddC) is the 2′,3′-dideoxy analogue of cytidine. Cytidine itself forms a stable hydrogel under the exact B(OH)₃ + AgNO₃ conditions listed (Ref. 6), but this process depends on coordination of the cis-2′,3′-diol to borate together with Ag⁺–nucleobase interactions that drive fiber formation. Removal of both hydroxyl groups (as in ddC) eliminates the borate-binding motif, so the same self-assembly pathway is unavailable.  

The closest experimental analogue is arabino-cytidine (Ref. 5), which also fails to gel under identical borate/Ag⁺ conditions; the change in sugar stereochemistry alone is sufficient to abolish gelation, and complete removal of the 2′/3′ OH groups is an even more disruptive modification.  

Although N⁴-acylated ddC derivatives can gel in EtOH/H₂O mixtures (Refs. 1–4), those experiments use an entirely different solvent system and rely on hydrophobic interactions of the acyl chains rather than sugar–borate complexation. The present aqueous borate/Ag⁺ condition therefore maps directly onto the cytidine/arabinocytidine precedents and is predicted to give no gel.
