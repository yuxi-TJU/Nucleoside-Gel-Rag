To determine the gelation potential of 5'-O-Lauroyluridine, we must analyze its molecular structure in the context of supramolecular gel chemistry.

### Analysis
5'-O-Lauroyluridine is a nucleoside derivative consisting of a hydrophilic uridine headgroup and a hydrophobic lauroyl (C12) chain. This amphiphilic architecture is a classic design for low-molecular-weight gelators (LMWGs). 

1.  **Hydrogen Bonding:** The uridine moiety provides multiple hydrogen-bonding sites (the imide group and the 2', 3'-hydroxyl groups). These sites are essential for the directional self-assembly required to form the 1D fibrous networks that entrap solvent.
2.  **Hydrophobic Interactions:** The long alkyl chain (lauroyl) provides the necessary hydrophobic driving force for stacking and aggregation. The balance between the hydrophilic nucleoside head and the hydrophobic tail is critical for solubility and self-assembly.
3.  **Solvent Compatibility:** 
    *   **Chloroform (CHCl₃):** This is a non-polar solvent that facilitates the formation of strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds between the uridine units by minimizing competition for hydrogen bonding. 5'-O-Lauroyluridine is known to form stable organogels in non-polar or moderately polar solvents like chloroform and benzene due to the effective stacking of the nucleobases and the hydrophobic interactions of the lipid tails.
    *   **Benzene:** Similar to chloroform, benzene is an aromatic solvent that supports the stacking of nucleobases (pi-pi stacking) and allows the lipid chains to organize, typically resulting in gelation for this class of amphiphilic nucleosides.

Given the structural features (nucleoside head + lipid tail) and the nature of the solvents provided, 5'-O-Lauroyluridine is a well-documented LMWG that forms stable gels in these organic media.
