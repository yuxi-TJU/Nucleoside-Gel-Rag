The target molecule isoGd is the 2′-deoxy analogue of isoG. Reference 5 shows that the ribo parent isoG readily forms stable hydrogels with LiCl via Li⁺-templated isoG quartets/pentaplexes that stack into a fibrillar network. Removal of the 2′-OH, however, eliminates a key stabilizing interaction (both H-bond donation and conformational rigidity), weakening the ability of the assemblies to resist crystallization or precipitation.

This pattern is confirmed by the closest structural analogue in the data set (z8isoGd, chemical distance 0.775). That molecule likewise fails to gel in 0.2 M LiCl and instead remains a solution or precipitate; the mechanistic explanation given is that Li⁺ lacks the optimal size/charge density to template the required stacked assemblies once the 2′-OH is absent. The same outcome is observed for the deoxyguanosine series (dG, reference 2), which crystallizes rather than gels under identical LiCl conditions.

Consequently, isoGd is predicted to behave analogously: the combination of 2′-deoxygenation and Li⁺ coordination is insufficient to produce a percolating hydrogel network.
