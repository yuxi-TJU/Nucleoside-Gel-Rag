The molecule 8-methoxyguanosine is a close structural analogue of guanosine itself. Guanosine and many of its C8-substituted derivatives are well-known to self-assemble into G-quadruplex-based hydrogels via Hoogsteen hydrogen-bonded G-quartets; these quartets are further stabilized by alkali-metal cations (especially K⁺) that occupy the central channel. The 8-methoxy substituent lies on the Hoogsteen face but does not abolish quartet formation; instead it modulates the syn/anti glycosidic torsion angle and slightly enhances solubility, both of which are compatible with gelation under heat-cool conditions.
