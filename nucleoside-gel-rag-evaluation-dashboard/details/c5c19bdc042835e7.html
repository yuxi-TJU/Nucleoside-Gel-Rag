The target molecule (z8isoGd) is a close structural analog of isoGd (Reference 1), which readily forms stable, transparent hydrogels with LiCl via self-assembly into cation-templated isoG quartets/pentaplexes that stack into a percolating fiber network. However, the 8-aza substitution introduces a decisive change: replacement of C8 by N lowers N7 basicity and thereby disrupts the Hoogsteen-edge hydrogen-bonding pattern that is essential for stable quartet/pentaplex formation in aqueous media.

The 2′-deoxy modification present in z8isoGd does not compensate for the loss of Hoogsteen bonding, nor does the use of LiCl overcome the hydrogen-bond competition from water. Consequently, the molecule remains in solution or forms only transient, non-network aggregates rather than a hydrogel.
