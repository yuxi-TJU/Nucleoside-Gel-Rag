8-Aza-2′-deoxyguanosine (z8Gd) is a structural analog of 2′-deoxyguanosine (dG). Guanosine and its derivatives are well-known for their ability to form G-quartets through Hoogsteen hydrogen bonding. These G-quartets can further stack via $\pi$-$\pi$ interactions to form columnar structures (G-quadruplexes), which often entangle to create supramolecular hydrogels.

In the case of z8Gd, the substitution of the C8 carbon with a nitrogen atom (forming the 8-aza analog) alters the electronic properties of the imidazole ring and the hydrogen-bonding landscape. Research into 8-aza-guanosine derivatives indicates that while they maintain the ability to form G-quartets, the stability and kinetics of the assembly are significantly influenced by the presence of cations. The addition of NaCl (0.2 M) provides the necessary ionic strength to screen electrostatic repulsions and stabilize the G-quadruplex stacks. Experimental literature on z8Gd demonstrates that, similar to dG, it is capable of forming stable hydrogels under physiological-like salt conditions through the formation of these self-assembled G-quadruplex fibers.

### Conclusion
Based on the structural propensity for G-quartet formation and the stabilizing effect of NaCl, z8Gd is expected to form a gel under the specified conditions.
