### Analysis
1.  **Molecular Components:** Uridine contains a ribose sugar moiety and a uracil base. The uracil base possesses a cis-diol group on the ribose and an imide group on the base.
2.  **Interaction Mechanism:** Boric acid is known to form reversible boronate ester complexes with the cis-diols of the ribose sugar. This cross-linking can facilitate the formation of supramolecular networks.
3.  **Role of Silver Ions:** Silver ions (Ag⁺) have a high affinity for the nitrogen atoms in the uracil base (specifically the N3 position). The coordination of Ag⁺ with the uracil base, combined with the boronate-mediated cross-linking of the ribose units, creates a multi-point coordination network.
4.  **Gelation Potential:** In basic conditions, the deprotonation of the uracil imide nitrogen (pKa ≈ 9.2) facilitates the coordination with Ag⁺. The combination of metal-ligand coordination (Ag-N) and boronate ester formation (B-O) provides sufficient structural rigidity and non-covalent cross-linking density to trap the solvent, leading to the formation of a metallo-supramolecular hydrogel. This specific system is a well-documented example of metal-ion-induced gelation in nucleoside chemistry.
