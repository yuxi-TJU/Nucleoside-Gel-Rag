The target molecule 8-methoxyguanosine is a close structural analogue of 8-hydroxyguanosine (Ref. 1; chemical distance 2.66). Both share the same guanosine core with an 8-position oxygen substituent; the only difference is methylation of that oxygen.  

G-quartet formation (the supramolecular motif required for gelation in guanosine derivatives) relies on unobstructed Hoogsteen-edge hydrogen bonding and an anti glycosidic torsion angle. The 8-oxygen substituent in both molecules sterically and electronically biases the nucleoside toward the syn conformation, thereby blocking the Hoogsteen face. Consequently, stable G-quartets do not assemble.  

This is directly confirmed by the experimental record for 8-hydroxyguanosine: addition of KCl (0.2 M) produces only a clear solution that never gels. The same salt and heat-cool protocol applied to 8-methoxyguanosine is therefore expected to give the identical outcome.  

Supporting observations from the other references reinforce the conclusion:  
- Xanthosine (Ref. 2), which also lacks a free Hoogsteen face, likewise fails to gel with KCl.  
- Only molecules that retain an unmodified Hoogsteen edge and can adopt the anti conformation (e.g., guanosine-5′-hydrazide, Ref. 3) gel with K⁺.  

Hence the target molecule does not form a gel in 5–354 mM KCl.
