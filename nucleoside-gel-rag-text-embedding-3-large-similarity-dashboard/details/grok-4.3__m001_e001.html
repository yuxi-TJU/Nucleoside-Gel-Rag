Transparent hydrogel, green Tb³⁺ emission (λem ≈ 545 nm); G′ ≈ 200 Pa; Transparent hydrogel, weak yellow Dy³⁺ emission; G′ ≈ 180 Pa

Each Tb³⁺/Dy³⁺ ion forms square-antiprismatic dG₄·Ln³⁺ clusters via O6/N7 and ribose hydroxyls. π–π stacking of guanine bases plus hydrogen-bond ladders extend the clusters into 1 D columns; lateral association yields nanofibrils only a few nm thick. Efficient guanine → Tb³⁺ energy transfer produces bright green luminescence; Dy³⁺ receives less energy, so emission is weaker. Acid, Cu²⁺ or F⁻ break coordination bonds, collapsing the network and quenching light, while neutralisation or EDTA reverses the process, evidencing coordination-driven, reversible gelation.
